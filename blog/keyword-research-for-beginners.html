Keyword Research for Beginners Using Only Free Tools

Keyword research is simply finding out what words real people type into Google when they need something you can help with. You do not need a paid tool to get started.

What a keyword really is

A keyword is not just a word. It is a question in disguise. "best running shoes flat feet" really means: "I have flat feet, my feet hurt, and I want a recommendation I can trust."

Free tools you can use

  • Google Autocomplete: start typing and note the suggestions.
  • People Also Ask: the expandable questions in search results.
  • Related searches: at the bottom of the results page.
  • Google Search Console: shows what you already appear for.
  • Google Trends: compares interest over time and by region.

A simple 5-step process

  1. List 5–10 seed topics your audience cares about.
  2. Expand each seed using Autocomplete and People Also Ask. Write every variation down.
  3. Group similar keywords into topical clusters. One page should target one main topic, not one exact phrase.
  4. Check the current results. Who is ranking? Big brands or small blogs? Can you do better?
  5. Prioritise: start with specific, longer phrases where the competition looks beatable.
💡 Beginner advantage: long, specific keywords have less competition and usually convert better, because the searcher knows exactly what they want.

Search intent matters most

Look at what Google already ranks for a keyword. If the top 10 are all step-by-step guides, writing a product page will not work. Match the format people expect:

  • Informational: "how to…", "what is…" → guides, tutorials
  • Commercial: "best…", "vs", "review" → comparisons
  • Transactional: "buy…", "price" → product or service pages
  • Navigational: a brand name → the brand's own site
Find the question. Understand the person asking it. Then write the best answer on the internet.
